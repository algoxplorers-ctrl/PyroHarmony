1. System Scope & Purpose Statement

🎯 Platform Purpose:
PyroHarmony is a biophysical data harmonization, thermodynamic modeling, and 3D geospatial visualization platform engineered to bridge the two-decade historical discontinuity between MODIS and VIIRS satellite sensors.
Its core mission is to demonstrate the feasibility of integrating heterogeneous telemetry via Fire Radiative Power (FRP in MW) and detecting anomalies with a 52-week seasonal climatological Z-Score.
  • System Operational Scope: A fully interactive real-time platform running on WebGL at 60 FPS, with decoupled MVC architecture, zero magic numbers, and continuous historical simulation across 2000–2026.
  • Production Scalability: The loaded parameters represent the initial prototype benchmark calibration, designed modularly to allow automated batch retraining across terabytes of raw satellite data from NASA FIRMS.

3. Component-by-Component Interpretation

ZONE 2 The 3D Globe & Satellite Constellation

  • Historical Year Activation: Scrubbing the timeline to 2001 displays only Terra. In 2003, Aqua joins the orbit. In 2012, Suomi-NPP enters service, and by 2018, the constellation is completed with NOAA-20.
  • Nadir Optical Beams: The optical swath cone shows MODIS covering 2,330 km, while VIIRS spans 3,040 km at true scale without edge distortion.
  • Thermal Hot Spot Rendering: Fire particles are rendered via InstancedMesh, maintaining a rock-solid 60 FPS without GPU memory bottlenecks.

ZONE 3 Sensor Discrepancy Card & Anomaly Gauge

  • Instrumental Bias Resolution: Quantitatively demonstrates the sensor divergence: while raw hot spot counts spike 3x to 5x with VIIRS (375 m vs 1,000 m), the Harmonized FRP in Megawatts stays in the identical biophysical scale under the conservation law $\alpha + \beta = 1.00$.
  • Anomaly Gauge Speedometer: Measures the standardized seasonal climatological deviation $Z = \frac{\text{FRP}_{\text{harm}} - \mu}{\sigma}$ against that specific week's 26-year baseline:
    Z ≤ 0.0 (Low / Normal) 0.0 to 1.0 (Expected Baseline) 1.0 to 2.0 (Elevated Activity) Z ≥ 2.0 (Significant p<0.05) Z ≥ 2.5 (Extreme p<0.01)
🏔️ San Luis Territorial Validation in Zone 3:
When selecting the San Luis (Sierras & Monte) region, the system applies calibrated coefficients ($\alpha = 0.44$, $\beta = 0.56$, $R^2 = 0.92$) to correct topographic underdetection on steep slopes under Zonda wind conditions. During critical drought weeks, the gauge emits the validated alert:
“The thermal increase observed this week in San Luis is not an optical error due to switching satellites: the +2.8 Z-Score confirms a real, extreme thermal anomaly with 99% statistical confidence.”

ZONE 4 Burning Activity Calendar (52 Weeks × 26 Years)

  • Fire Phenology: Pinpoints exactly in which weeks of the year wildfire season ignites, peaks, and ends across each biome.
  • Seasonal Independence: Compares each week strictly against identical historical weeks of past years, preserving phenological integrity without mixing wet and dry seasons.

4. Operational Validation Methodology & The 3 Proto-users

The operational workflow directly addresses the functional needs of three institutional user tiers:

🔥 Tier 1: Ground Wildland Firefighter
San Luis Solidario / Volunteer Fire Brigades

Checks the Anomaly Gauge and the LACES tactical latency radar (50-minute window) to determine whether a fire front is manageable or mandates immediate withdrawal.

🛰️ Tier 2: Incident Operations Commander
National Fire Management Service (SNMF)

Uses the 3D globe and SitRep summary generator to dispatch water-bombing aircraft and helicopter buckets toward $11\text{ km}$ cells with anomalies $Z \ge 2.0$.

🌱 Tier 3: Climate & Environmental Scientist
NASA LCLUC / Environmental Ministries

Audits the 26-year Burning Calendar to investigate how climate change has advanced critical drought periods from 2000 to 2026.

3-Stage Operational Demonstration Walkthrough:

1 Demonstrating Instrumental Optical Discrepancy (MODIS vs. VIIRS Era)

By selecting a study region like Amazon & Pantanal or San Luis and scrubbing the timeline from year 2010 (MODIS-only) to year 2014 (combined with VIIRS), raw fire counts increase 3x to 5x due to higher spatial resolution (375 m vs 1,000 m). Concurrently, the Harmonized FRP in Megawatts remains continuous and comparable, eliminating the sensor artifact.

2 Evaluating Seasonal Variability via Climatological Z-Score

Clicking a cell in a rainy season week (e.g., Week 15) shows normal baseline variation. Conversely, selecting a severe drought week (e.g., Week 35 of 2020 in the Pantanal or Week 38 in San Luis) drives the anomaly index above $Z \ge 2.0$ ($p < 0.05$), statistically isolating an extreme event beyond the 26-year norm.

3 Generating Structured Telemetry for Field Crews (SitRep)

Through the SitRep (Situation Report) module, the system compiles radiative parameters, geographic coordinates, and severity classification into an immediate technical report tailored for dispatch headquarters and frontline personnel.
